Fig. X. Three-dimensional shapes of the Mab light-echo structure are consistent with a single common shape.

Each point is one independent sky window, fitted separately: the ellipsoidal second-order structure function of the reconstructed three-dimensional emissivity gives three principal axes $a_1 \ge a_2 \ge a_3$, plotted as the two independent axis ratios. Both ratios are lengths of the lag vector at fixed $S_2$, so they are independent of the structure-function slope. Error bars are $\pm1\sigma$ from block jackknife ($k = 4$); on a logarithmic axis a symmetric bar reaches the axis floor where the uncertainty exceeds the value. Both axes stop at unity, the physical limit of each ratio, so the top and right edges are the prolate ($a_3 = a_2$) and oblate ($a_2 = a_1$) degenerate loci and their corner is a sphere; error bars reaching the limit are truncated there. Windows are split at the quartiles of their echo signal-to-noise ratio (top S/N quartile 4.9–9.5; second quartile 2.5–4.8; lower half 1.4–2.4) and drawn back to front, so the best-measured quartile is never hidden. The grey diagonal is $a_2/a_1 = a_3/a_2$, on which the two ratios are equal: windows above it are the more prolate (cigar-like), those below the more oblate (pancake-like), while the marked edges are the limits at which an ellipsoid becomes exactly prolate or exactly oblate.

The blue diamond is the maximum-likelihood common shape of the highest signal-to-noise quartile, $a_2/a_1 = 0.25$, $a_3/a_2 = 0.54$. The dashed ellipse is the null hypothesis that every window has exactly this one shape and the observed spread is measurement error alone; its semi-axes are the median measurement uncertainties (0.303 and 0.092 dex) and it contains 4 of 28 windows in that quartile (14%) against the 39.4% a two-dimensional $1\sigma$ contour expects, so the null is rejected. The solid filled ellipse adds an intrinsic window-to-window scatter of $\sigma_\mathrm{int} = 0.151$ and 0.140 dex in the two ratios in quadrature with those uncertainties; it is the only contour directly comparable with the plotted points, which carry both terms, and it contains 9 of 28 (32%). The intrinsic scatter is thus a small fraction of a decade: the windows differ measurably in shape, but around one shape rather than across the plane.

Of 115 windows fitted, 112 returned a usable fit and 94 of those converged to a non-degenerate ellipsoid; axes are additionally clipped below 0.08, where the ratios are not trusted, leaving 89 drawn. Legend counts follow the drawing; the maximum-likelihood shape uses every usable window in the highest quartile (29), while the coverage fractions above are measured on the 28 of those that converged to a non-degenerate ellipsoid. One highest-quartile window (r3200_c800) falls below the axis floor and is not drawn; it is retained in the fit. 4 further windows from the lower tiers fall below the floor.
